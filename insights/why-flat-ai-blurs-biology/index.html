Why Flat AI Blurs Biology, And How A Curved Map Keeps The Sign

Instructional Biology

Ask most graph neural networks what happens when one signal switches a receptor on and another switches it off, and you get an average: nothing. That answer is wrong, and it is wrong in the most important place, because medicine lives in the push and pull between signals. This is the problem ASAP was designed around.

Over-Smoothing: When Signals Blur

A graph neural network learns by passing messages between neighboring nodes. Stack enough rounds of that and every node starts to look like its neighbors, until distinct signals blur into one. Researchers call this over-smoothing (Li, Han and Wu, AAAI 2018). In biology it is worse than a loss of detail: a link that lowers a signal gets averaged against a link that raises it, and an inhibitor can end up read as an activator.

Fix One: Give Every Branch Its Own Room

ASAP places its map in a curved, hyperbolic space, where room grows exponentially toward the edge, so branches that fan out to thousands of outcomes do not collide. Graph neural networks that work in this space were introduced in the research literature as hyperbolic graph convolutional networks (Chami and colleagues, NeurIPS 2019).

Fix Two: Keep The Sign

Room alone does not stop a "raises" and a "lowers" from cancelling. ASAP's network carries the sign of every link. Instead of subtracting an inhibitory message, it turns the update by its own learned angle, so a blocking signal points somewhere distinct and is never mistaken for an absent one. We call this signed, anisotropic message passing: every kind of link is weighted and turned in its own way.

Fix Three: Treat Loops As Feedback

Biology is full of loops: a hormone suppresses its own release, a drug provokes the body to compensate. ASAP does not force loops into the geometry of its map. They go into message passing and into its homeostatic-rebellion score, which asks how strongly the body would push back, through compensation, rebound or tolerance, against a proposed change.

The Result

With all three in place, ASAP recovered the direction of 89.0% of hidden links with 90% of its training links hidden, in a 128-dimensional map whose distortion stayed within the patent application's 0.05 gate. Read what the trained map contains, or explore the reasoning system.
