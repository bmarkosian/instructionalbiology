Medicine Made For One: From The Average Patient To n = 1

Instructional Biology

Every medicine is approved on an average, and nobody is the average. In a widely cited Nature commentary, Nicholas Schork reported that the ten highest-grossing drugs in the United States help between 1 in 25 and 1 in 4 of the people who take them (Schork, Nature 2015). A positive trial can hide who actually benefited. Instructional Medicine starts from the other end: one person.

A Twin For Every Patient

A patient digital twin is a living computer model of one person, updated continuously by real-world data. Just as engineers test a virtual turbine before running the real engine, the twin projects a person's likely response before a dose changes. It reads consumer wearables, such as a smartwatch or ring, calibrated against a physician's clinical reference sensors, and compares what it predicted with what it measures. It ignores brief fluctuations and flags a divergence only when a short-term change persists alongside a long-term trend.

The twin draws on ASAP, our 128-dimensional map of signaling biology, to work out which signals to adjust and in which direction.

The Physician Sets The Limits

Every adjustment stays inside limits a licensed physician sets in advance. A safety controller in the device checks each command, can always reduce or pause a dose, and falls back to a safe baseline if data is lost. Any increase beyond the limits needs the physician's approval. A wellness version for consumers is display-only: it informs the person's own choices and has no control over dosing.

From n = 1 To A Plausible Mechanism

In November 2025, FDA leaders described a Plausible Mechanism Pathway for bespoke, individualized therapies in the New England Journal of Medicine (summary from AABB). We intend to pursue it for individualized therapies. Qualification is not yet established.

Human evidence on similar material already exists. A published 12-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (Morita and Hirano, Front. Nutr. 2025; 102 participants) found that a porcine extract separated from placebo on its primary mood endpoint (p = 0.044). We call the link from that study to our platform the Morita Bridge: once comparability with our material is shown, the Bridge carries that human evidence to our platform.

Virtual Cohorts

Many twins together form a virtual cohort, which can project the spread of responses before a physical trial is run, so the trials that do run ask sharper questions.

Where It Stands

Our patient digital twin runs today on simulated subjects and has not yet been validated on patients. It is protected as one of five independent systems in our pending U.S. patent application, and it is the part of our platform that brings everything else, from native-protein manufacturing to exosome delivery, to one person. Talk to us about licensing digital-twin dosing.
