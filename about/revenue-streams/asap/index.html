ASAP

Instructional Biology

ASAP, the Advanced Science AI Platform, is Instructional Biology's discovery engine. It reasons over the Signaling Cascade Knowledge Graph, a hyperbolic map of peptides, receptors, signaling cascades, master regulators, disease states and outcomes, and the typed, directional relationships between them. We offer ASAP to pharma and biotech partners in three forms: subscriptions, discovery partnerships and platform licenses.

Where It Stands

ASAP is at Ready for Demo, the fifth of the seven stages on Our Pipeline: Discovery, Development, Testing, Prototype, Ready for Demo, IND (if required) and Sales. Of our ten revenue streams, only our OTC products, already at Sales, are further along.

Its knowledge graph is now frozen as the final record of November 4, 2026: 12,835 nodes and 35,004 edges built from 19,519 PubMed papers and 11,537 clinical trials, on a trained 128-dimensional Poincaré ball (mean distortion 0.0469, within the 0.05 gate). It has made 19,251 new predicted-benefit calls across 2,409 diseases and disease phenotypes, and 20 demo patient digital twins (simulated subjects) run on it. These are computational results, not clinical results.

What It Does

ASAP takes a large, messy body of biological evidence and organizes it into a structure a computational model can reason over explicitly, instead of holding it informally in one researcher's working memory. Two design choices make that possible. The graph is embedded in hyperbolic space, a geometry that accommodates the exponential branching of a real biological hierarchy without running out of room at the periphery the way flat layouts do. And the reasoning layer uses graph neural network message passing that tracks polarity, so a peptide that activates a pathway and one that suppresses it are never collapsed into one ambiguous signal.

Given a biological question, ASAP returns a ranked set of hypotheses: connections between a signaling molecule and a possible outcome, weighted by how well the connecting evidence supports them and how central the intermediate biology is to the wider network. Every output traces back to the evidence behind it and is reviewed by a person before it goes anywhere near a laboratory bench. ASAP does not produce a diagnosis, a treatment decision or a claim of efficacy. The Poincaré Explorer shows the geometry live.

Why It Sits at the Center

ASAP is the operating system behind everything else we build: the ISRS-ML module, the retrofit bioreactor skids, the MET-APFS (Markosian Exosomal Transcytosis - Autonomic Pharmacokinetic Feedback System) targeting system and the personalized-medicine digital twin. Every safety stop runs in hardware on its own, so if ASAP ever goes silent, the process stops safely. ASAP also powers a second revenue stream, In-Silico Tox & Clinical, where it works together with the digital twin.

The Segment That Protects It

ASAP is protected by the ASAP AI segment of our IP portfolio. The system is claimed in U.S. Patent Application No. 19/793,487 (claim 18). The claims are pending and have not been examined.
