Why The Portfolio Was Built

The IP portfolio for Instructional Biology was meticulously crafted to ensure that this new field of medicine cannot be impeded by big pharma, bureaucracy, and conflicting interests. That's why I carefully wrote every word, and drafted the complete vertically integrated platform, with 10 revenue streams along 160 continuation applications fully described in my omnibus non-provisional patent application, ensuring that the future of medicine would reach the people who need it most, rather than those who put profit ahead of the well-being and welfare of others.

For me, this is personal, as I've been offered money to buy one of my patents, only to learn that the potential buyer wanted to shelve the technology rather than develop it since, "the money's in the treatment, not the cure." I disagree, and I did not sell. Instructional Medicine is a paradigm shift, and represents the future of our medical system; therefore, I wanted to secure the entire field, from every possible angle, before competing interests learn about what I've been developing in stealth over the years.

My goal is to provide real cures, not temporary treatments, and deliver them to people who need them now, without prioritizing financial interests above the welfare of others. The technology I developed is open for third-party licensing, and was designed to be horizontally licensed across the entire platform, however the vertical integration means that Instructional Biology will have everything needed to control its fate, irrespective of big pharma, potential competition, or necessary licensed technologies to complete the company's ability to create a platform from discovery to delivery.

— Boris Markosian, Founder and Sole Inventor

How The Portfolio Is Built

The parent application claims the five systems of Instructional Medicine as five independent inventions: ASAP discovery, MHFBP bio-processing with the Thermodynamic Cold-Stop, ISRS-ML pathogen reduction, the MET-APFS (Markosian Exosomal Transcytosis - Autonomic Pharmacokinetic Feedback System) carrier, and digital twin regulation.

From that parent, planned continuations and continuations-in-part extend protection across ten commercial segments, from over-the-counter products to regulated disease indications. 451 applications are drafted: 160 continuations and 291 continuations-in-part. 6 applications are filed.

The systems stand on their own as well as together. ISRS-ML pathogen reduction, the MET-APFS carrier, ASAP and the digital twin do not depend on the bio-processing claims, so a company can license any one of them alone.
