The Human Simulator

The patient digital twin in five minutes: how a living model of one person tests a dose before it reaches the bloodstream.

About This Film

The patient digital twin in five minutes: how a living model of one person is designed to test a dose before it reaches the bloodstream. The figures on the dashboard in the film come from an earlier October build of our knowledge graph. Platform overview: forward-looking statements, not a claim of clinical results.

Where This Film Appears

  • The Patient Digital Twin: A Flight Simulator for One Body

  • Therapeutic-Regulation System

Transcript

0:01 Before a modern jet engine ever leaves the factory floor or takes to the sky, it goes through thousands of hours of testing. But that testing doesn't happen on a runway. It happens inside a computer. Engineers build what they call a digital twin, a highly detailed living computational model of that exact engine.

0:22 As real-world data flows in, the digital model updates, letting builders see exactly how the machine will perform under intense stress. Now, think about the human body. Our biological systems are vastly more complicated than any machine we have ever engineered. Yet, when it comes to treating illness, we rely on a surprisingly outdated approach.

0:43 We give patients standard, average doses based on general guidelines, and then we simply wait to see how they react. Look at this visual comparison. On the left, we map out every stress point of an engine before we push it to the limit. On the right, we treat human patients using educated guesswork, hoping for the best.

1:02 A company called Instructional Biology is working to close that gap. They are building the equivalent of a flight simulator, but for human medicine. To get to a place where medical treatments are truly safe and customized for you, we have to stop testing on the physical body first. We need a way to test treatments on a digital model of the patient beforehand.

1:22 Creating that kind of simulator is a massive challenge. Before you can predict how one specific person will react, you first have to map out the entire biological system they belong to. This is where the Advanced Science AI Platform, or ASAP, comes in. Think of ASAP as a master blueprint of human biological signaling, charting out how our cells communicate and function.

1:46 This dashboard displays the scale of information powering that blueprint. Metrics on the right show findings synthesized from 6,000 published papers, generating 50,000 mapped predictions. On the left, a colorful circular map organizes this data into an interconnected web, showing how disease and healing pathways interact. As comprehensive as this master blueprint is, it still has one limitation.

2:12 It represents an average human baseline, pulling from broad population data. Since no two patients share the exact same biology, a static average blueprint is not enough to confidently cure a specific individual. That broad map is just the starting point. The next step is translating that general biology into a highly personalized model.

2:33 To do that, the system warps the baseline blueprint. It pulls in your specific genetic profile, your metabolism and your medical history, reshaping the standard model to match you perfectly. A true digital twin is an active living model. Instead of a static snapshot from your last doctor's visit, it constantly updates to reflect changes in your body right now.

2:56 This diagram shows how that connection is made. In the bottom right section, you see a wearable biosensor on the patient's arm. That sensor acts as a bridge, feeding real-time data from the physical patient straight back into their digital model. With that live connection established, a doctor can now run a precise simulation.

3:15 They can test a specific dose on your digital twin and watch exactly how your unique biology will react. And the most important part, all of this simulation happens before a single drop of medicine ever enters your actual bloodstream. By keeping the data looping between the patient and the twin, we can spot adverse reactions early. We shift the entire trial and error process out of the hospital bed and into the digital realm.

3:41 Placing so much trust in a simulation requires strict safety boundaries. The AI system maps the pathways and proposes treatments, but human physicians always review the evidence, set the hard limits and make the final decisions. There is also a physical fail-safe built directly into the delivery process. If the wearable sensor detects your body reacting in a way that falls outside those doctor-approved limits, the device immediately halts the flow of medicine.

4:08 Now imagine taking this level of individual precision and scaling it up. What happens when you have thousands of these digital twins running at the same time? You get virtual cohorts. Instead of recruiting thousands of people for a physical clinical trial and waiting years for the results, researchers can test a new drug across a massive grid of unique digital twins to predict its success almost instantly.

4:34 This workflow illustrates the complete loop. The insights from those digital simulations guide how biological medicines are manufactured, made safe and finally delivered. As shown by the dotted line at the bottom, the data from that delivery flows directly back to the beginning to inform the next round of discovery. By mapping biological signals and pairing them with real-time digital testing, Instructional Biology replaces standard average doses with precise calculation.

5:03 We finally arrive at a point where medical treatment is built, tested and delivered exclusively for you.
