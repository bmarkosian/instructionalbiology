Instructional Medicine

A plain-language overview of Instructional Medicine and the patent-pending platform behind it.

About This Film

A plain-language overview of Instructional Medicine and the patent-pending platform behind it: forward-looking statements, simplified illustrations, not medical advice.

Where This Film Appears

  • Home

Transcript

0:01 Have you ever gotten a paper cut, gone about your week, and looked down a few days later to see it completely healed? How exactly did your body know to do that? That cellular repair doesn't happen by accident. It is directed by natural signaling proteins.

0:18 You can think of them as tiny, highly specific messengers carrying instructions, telling your cells exactly when to calm inflammation and how to rebuild the tissue. When we get sick and need outside help, we don't usually turn to those natural messengers. For over a hundred years, modern medicine has relied almost entirely on synthetic chemistry. We invent completely new, artificial molecules in a laboratory.

0:44 These chemicals are built from scratch, entirely outside of the delicate, natural biochemical language your body uses to communicate with itself. We have essentially treated the human body like a simple machine with mechanical switches, ignoring the rich, coordinated language of healing it already speaks. Looking at this diagram, legacy chemical pharmacology uses a man-made chemical to forcefully lock onto a single biological receptor. But your body is an interconnected network.

1:13 When you forcefully pin down just one switch, the surrounding systems notice. The network reacts, trying to route around the blockage. As the body fights back against this forced chemical command, patients build tolerance and often experience severe side effects. We see the starkest cost of treating human biology this way in the opioid crisis, where forcefully blocking pain pathways causes the nervous system to heavily adapt and resist.

1:40 Whenever a synthetic drug aggressively coerces an isolated part of your biology, the rest of your dynamic network will inevitably push back to regain balance. This brings us to a completely different approach, called Instructional Medicine. Instead of inventing new foreign chemicals to force the body into submission, this method captures the natural biologics your body already produces to heal itself. Rather than isolating one single target, Instructional Medicine looks at the whole picture.

2:10 It gathers entire, coordinated teams of these signaling proteins that naturally work together in harmony to achieve a goal, like reducing swelling or repairing tissue. The specific group of signals your body relies on to direct this repair is known as the Thermosensitive Peptidome. Think of this collection like a detailed repair manual, providing the exact instructions your cells need to heal. To actually restore balance, the body needs this complete set of biological instructions acting together, rather than a single chemical hammer trying to control the entire system.

2:45 If our own bodies make the perfect medicine, you might wonder why we haven't simply collected these natural proteins and used them as therapies before. The answer comes down to how we manufacture drugs. To make medicine safe, traditional pharmaceutical factories rely on extreme heat for sterilization. Heat is harmless to a rugged synthetic chemical, but it is devastating to the fragile biology of natural proteins.

3:10 A protein's ability to send a message depends entirely on its precise, three-dimensional folded shape. When you apply high heat, that delicate shape unravels. The protein melts into a useless string, and the biological instruction is completely destroyed. To solve this, a company called Instructional Biology engineered a completely new way to make medicine.

3:31 They built a system from the ground up to isolate these fragile proteins and clear away harmful pathogens without ever using high-temperature sterilization. Looking at the right side of this diagram, you can see their connected manufacturing stack. Step 1 shows how the system gently extracts the native proteins without altering their delicate structure. Step 2 then uses a specialized, light-based technology to safely clear away pathogens without applying any harmful heat.

4:00 Because these natural proteins are kept safely away from extreme temperatures, they retain their complex, folded shapes. The crucial messages they carry survive intact, from the moment they are discovered all the way to the moment they are given to a patient. By removing heat from the entire manufacturing pipeline, we can finally collect, preserve, and use the body's own complex biological software. This table lays out exactly how a century of synthetic drugs compares to the future of Instructional Medicine.

4:30 In the first row, we move away from pushing one man-made molecule onto a single target and instead use the body's own signaling proteins, which are designed to work together as a network. Next, we stop relying on a fixed dose, calculated for a statistical average patient. Instead, dosing is tailored precisely to the individual, monitored safely within a doctor's limits. And critically, as shown in the third row, we abandon manufacturing processes that use heat and destroy the very shape that makes the medicine work.

5:00 The new standard keeps every protein perfectly folded and safely verified. The most capable pharmacy has been inside of us the entire time. We just needed the right end-to-end technology to carefully extract those signals, preserve them, and deliver them back where they are needed. The human body operates on a rich, dynamic language of biological instructions.

5:21 By learning to capture and speak that language, we are finally creating medicine made by the body for the body.
