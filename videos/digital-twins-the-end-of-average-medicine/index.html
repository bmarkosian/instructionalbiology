Digital Twins: The End of Average Medicine

How a patient digital twin replaces one-size-fits-all dosing with a model of one person, updated from wearable data and kept inside clinician-set limits.

About This Film

How a patient digital twin is designed to replace one-size-fits-all dosing with a model of one person, updated from wearable data and kept inside clinician-set limits. Platform overview: forward-looking statements, not a claim of clinical results.

Where This Film Appears

  • Digital Twins: A Model That Learns With You

  • Patient Twin

Transcript

0:01 Did you know that some of the top-grossing medications on the market today only actually help between 1 in 25 and 1 in 4 of the people taking them? This happens because we design our medicines for a mathematical average. We calculate what works for a broad population and prescribe that middle-ground dose to everyone. The problem is, an entirely average person does not actually exist.

0:27 Look at this scatterplot of patient responses. Individual lines represent the average result for the placebo group and the treated group. On paper, the treatment line looks like a success. But look at the individual dots scattered everywhere.

0:40 Many people taking the medication see no meaningful change at all. When we build a medical system that treats a mathematical average, we guarantee that we are going to miss the actual individual sitting in the doctor's office. Right now, standard medicine relies on a static snapshot. Your doctor takes your vitals during a short clinic visit and uses that single moment in time to set a fixed dose for you to take over the next few weeks or months.

1:06 Imagine trying to navigate rush hour city traffic using a paper map printed 5 years ago. You would constantly hit roadblocks because your static map doesn't know about current construction or accidents. Human biology works the exact same way. Your body is not a fixed piece of paper.

1:23 It fluctuates constantly throughout the day, across different daily rhythms, hormonal cycles, and inflammatory responses. A static dose quickly drifts completely out of alignment with what your body actually needs at that moment. To successfully treat a moving biological target, medicine requires a live GPS navigation system, not a static paper map. That live navigation system is called a biological digital twin.

1:50 It functions as a living, continuously evolving computational model of your specific biology, grounded entirely in your real-world medical data rather than science fiction concepts of digital cloning. It starts with a broad baseline map of how a population responds to a treatment. Then that map is warped and adjusted to perfectly fit your unique genetics, your metabolism, in your personal clinical history. By doing this, the system translates an abstract study of 1,000 strangers into a deeply personal, highly accurate profile of you.

2:26 To keep that model alive, you wear a biosensor, like a smartwatch or ring, that constantly streams your real-time physical signs back to the twin as you go about your normal day. Look at the patient digital twin and delivery panel. The system compares the expected biological path against what your wearable measures. When a gap opens, it recalculates your next dose, like GPS rerouting traffic.

2:51 But it lacks unchecked control. Notice the cryptographic safety envelope on the left. This is an authorization envelope. Your human doctor signs off on strict minimum and maximum limits for your medication.

3:05 The software is only allowed to drive safely inside those pre-approved boundaries. This creates a closed-loop system. You receive highly responsive, bespoke healing tailored to your exact physical state, while your physician remains entirely in the driver's seat. The benefits of this approach extend far beyond your own health.

3:27 Every time your digital twin learns from how you respond in the real world, it sends that evidence back to the researchers discovering new medicines. When you group many of these individual digital models together, you form a virtual cohort. This lets researchers test proposed treatments against a wide variety of unique biological profiles simultaneously. By running simulations through this virtual network first, researchers can compute the exact spread of individual responses before a physical clinical trial begins.

3:59 They can identify exactly who a drug will help, and who it won't, before a single patient takes a pill, rather than discovering those failures years down the line. This is a form of medicine that tracks your individual response, speaks your native biological language, and learns continuously with you. We no longer have to base our healthcare on an imaginary, mathematical middle ground. As this technical summary states, digital twins represent the end of average medicine.

4:30 By tracking and adapting to your exact physical state, we can finally build a treatment plan made specifically for you. Medicine Made By the Body, For the Body. www.instructionalbiology.com
