Athermal Sterilization: ISRS-ML

How ISRS-ML clears pathogens with light in continuous flow, without the heat that damages delicate biologics.

About This Film

Why heat-based pathogen clearance damages delicate biologics, and how ISRS-ML clears pathogens with light in continuous flow, so product can be released on real-time data instead of waiting weeks in quarantine. Platform overview: forward-looking statements, not a claim of clinical results.

Where This Film Appears

  • Pathogen-Reduction System

Transcript

0:01 We are looking at the dividing line in modern bio-manufacturing. On one side, traditional thermal processing applies aggressive heat to clear pathogens. On the other, continuous flow optics achieved the exact same clearance using light. Applying heat reliably neutralizes viruses, but that exact same thermal energy denatures the delicate three-dimensional structures of therapeutic proteins.

0:26 This physical limitation severely impacts the most advanced biological treatments. Process engineers face a direct contradiction. Delivering lethal energy to destroy contaminant risks destroying the therapeutic payload right along with it. Until production facilities can destroy pathogens at scale without heat, manufacturing throughput remains strictly capped by the physical fragility of the product itself.

0:50 To avoid heat, the industry relies on severe chemical and mechanical workarounds, pushing the product through acidic low pH shifts or forcing it through size-exclusion nanofilters. These methods introduce their own strict vulnerabilities. Filters can clog or shear large molecules under pressure, and chemical shifts require neutralizing buffers, demanding massive validation efforts for every new batch. The heaviest penalty is logistical.

1:19 Because these athermal steps are difficult to verify during active processing, facilities must lock highly perishable biological matrices in quarantine for weeks while waiting for retrospective infectivity assays from the lab. A short shelf-life cell or plasma product degrading in a holding tank represents massive financial waste and directly delays critical doses reaching patients. Instructional Biology has engineered a continuous flow alternative. By combining impulsive stimulated Raman scattering with a machine learning controller, called ISRS-ML, the system delivers a complete athermal clearance mechanism.

1:58 This shifts quality assurance away from retrospective laboratory delays. The regulatory and commercial value of this tightly controlled physical process lies in eliminating the post-batch assay hold entirely. The standard legacy steps include inducing a severe low pH chemical shift, and physically pushing the fluid through a virus-retentive size-exclusion nanofilter. We insert the ISRS-ML module directly into the center of this cascade, acting as a uniquely optical and acoustic barrier.

2:29 Because the technology is severable, contract manufacturers can plug this specific optical step directly into existing production chains. ISRS-ML resolves the central bio-manufacturing paradox.
