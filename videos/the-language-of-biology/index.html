The Language of Biology: The Boris Markosian Story

How Boris Markosian's path through the lab and through data led him to Instructional Medicine, and the patent-pending platform he filed as sole inventor.

About This Film

Boris Markosian's path from physiology and neuroscience at UC San Diego, cancer research at the UCSD Moores Cancer Center and Genelux, and the Salk Institute, to data operations at Google, and how it led to Instructional Medicine and the patent-pending platform he filed as sole inventor. Platform overview: forward-looking statements, not a claim of clinical results.

Where This Film Appears

  • Founder's Story

Transcript

0:01 For the last century, medical science has largely treated the human body like a machine. If a part isn't working, we invent a synthetic chemical in a lab and use it to force a receptor into submission. But this mechanical approach carries a massive human cost. When you treat complex biological systems as simple chemical equations, you get catastrophic failures.

0:26 The opioid epidemic is a devastating example of what happens when we try to bludgeon the body's receptors into compliance. There is a hidden flaw in how we make modern biologic drugs too. Traditional manufacturing relies on heat to sterilize and process materials. But biological proteins are delicate three-dimensional structures.

0:46 When you expose them to heat, they denature. They literally unfold and fall apart. In biology, form is function. If you destroy a protein's physical shape during processing, you destroy its ability to deliver the right message to the patient.

1:02 Boris Markosian looks at the body entirely differently. He argues the human body isn't a collection of independent mechanical parts to be blocked or forced. It is a highly interconnected language of signals. This perspective is the foundation of a new therapeutic field called Instructional Medicine.

1:21 Instead of introducing foreign chemicals, the goal is to heal the body by using the natural signaling proteins it already produces, isolating them with their physical structures completely intact. But translating that biological language isn't simple. To crack it, you need someone who understands the physical realities of handling delicate organic tissue, and who can also build the computational architecture required to map millions of molecular interactions. Markosian represents that rare cross-disciplinary overlap.

1:53 His career spans both intensive laboratory science and massive data operations, positioning him to translate the instructions the medical establishment has overlooked. Markosian's understanding of physical biology started with his studies in physiology and neuroscience at UC San Diego. From there, he moved into high-stakes wet lab environments, conducting cancer immunotherapy research at the UCSD Moores Cancer Center, and working on oncolytic virotherapy as a scientist at Genelux. He also gained crucial operational experience managing complex laboratory environments at the Salk Institute for Biological Studies.

2:32 Spending a decade handling delicate organic materials showed him firsthand exactly why traditional processing methods fail. He saw how standard techniques structurally destroy the very signaling molecules researchers are trying to study. Then he did something unusual for a biological researcher. He stepped out of the laboratory entirely and took a role as a data operations lead at Google.

2:56 In this corporate tech environment, he learned how to manage, map, and scale impossibly complex information architectures. He was learning how to organize massive data sets, skills directly applicable to the millions of signaling pathways in the human body. He first attempted to merge biology and data by founding Vaxenta Biotechnologies at Johnson & Johnson's JLabs, focusing on a cancer vaccine thesis. Later, he launched Placenta Products LLC, bringing an oral supplement to market to test the practical delivery of biological signaling molecules.

3:32 These early ventures proved a crucial concept. Biological signals carry vital information that standard chemical and nutritional models completely miss. 15 years of research recently culminated in a massive 2026 master intellectual property portfolio. Markosian prepared and filed it himself as the sole inventor, without outside legal counsel.

3:56 The portfolio includes six patent applications, built from over 200 drafts, designed to lay the groundwork for an entirely new therapeutic ecosystem. The first half of this system is computational. It's called the Advanced Science AI Platform, or ASAP, and it's designed to map exactly how the human body reasons on a molecular level. Take a look at these pathway diagrams.

4:19 This is ASAP's Signaling Cascade Knowledge Graph. It tracks how specific biological ingredients trigger direct outcomes. On the left, glowing connective lines map the pathway from an ingredient through receptors to an indication, like a diabetic foot ulcer. On the right, a similar route maps to wound healing.

4:41 Notice the text at the bottom. Standard databases only list facts, but this engine applies hyperbolic geometry to actively forecast disease directions, tracking every prediction back to its source literature. Because before you can deliver a natural biological instruction to a patient, you must first have the advanced computational map required to know which instruction to send. Once the right instruction is identified, the second half of the system has to physically extract it.

5:08 This is the Markosian High-Fidelity Bio-Processing Protocol. To solve the denaturing problem, this protocol utilizes a proprietary Thermodynamic Cold-Stop. It isolates heat-labile signaling peptides without ever using the destructive heat that ruins standard biologics. Markosian has engineered the two necessary halves, the computational brain to find the exact biological signals, and the physical engine to extract them with their structures perfectly intact.

5:37 The long-term objective here is to move away from mass-produced synthetic chemicals that treat the average patient, who doesn't actually exist. Instead, the goal is bespoke therapeutics, dosed and tailored to the unique biology of the individual. The platform's future involves payload-agnostic engineered exosomes, which will safely carry these natural biologics directly to where the body needs them. Look at this illustration.

6:05 It perfectly represents this new approach, matching data-driven computation on the left with natural, complex protein structures on the right. It captures the body's messages in their original form, resulting in medicine that is endogenous by design. It all points back to Markosian's core philosophy. The body already knows how to heal itself.

6:27 We simply provide the reminder. Sometimes a genuine medical shift requires someone who sits outside the traditional silos, a cross-disciplinary pioneer willing to finally learn and speak the language the medical establishment has forgotten.
